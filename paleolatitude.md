# Laurentia paleolatitude through time

The poles in this compilation imply a paleolatitude for any locality on Laurentia through time. The figures below track the paleolatitude of Duluth, Minnesota (46.79°N, 92.10°W) — on the southern margin of the Superior province — implied by each pole, following the top panel of Figure 4.5 of [Swanson-Hysell (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-818533-9.00009-6). Markers are colored and shaped by terrane and reliability grade; vertical bars are A{sub}`95` and horizontal bars are the age uncertainty. Poles uncertain by 50 Myr or more are omitted.

The compilation spans 1800 Ma to the present. Precambrian poles are the site-level recreations from the 2022 Kringerdalen and 2026 Iloranta workshops; Phanerozoic poles are the Laurentia subset of the Torsvik et al. (2012) compilation. Bands mark the durations of Nuna (1820–1380 Ma), Rodinia (1060–718 Ma), and Pangea (320–175 Ma).

Detrital remanence is shallowed during compaction, so a sedimentary pole taken as measured implies a paleolatitude that is a minimum. The sedimentary units are therefore plotted here at their inclination-shallowing-corrected positions, with the vertical bar taken from the equal-area circular approximation to the 95% confidence ellipse of the Kent distribution that carries the flattening uncertainty. Where the data permit, the flattening factor *f* is determined from the directional distribution of the unit itself by the elongation/inclination method of [Tauxe and Kent (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1029/145GM08) or by SVEI; where they do not, *f* is resampled from the compilation of measured flattening factors of [Pierce et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022GC010682). Each correction, and the as-measured pole it replaces, is tabulated in `data/nordic_summaries/kent_poles_combined.csv` and documented in that unit's assessment notebook. The 2017 comparison panel below is left as Evans et al. (2021) published it, so the two panels together show what correcting for compaction shallowing does to the Tonian and Mesoproterozoic record.
