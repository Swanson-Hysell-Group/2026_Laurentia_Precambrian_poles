Its three main intrusive units (Lalonde and Martin, 1983) range from dark green feldspar porphyry-hornblende syenite (unit 1) through grey hornblende syenite (unit 2) to trachytic quartz syenite (unit 3); all are cut by aplite and microsyenite dykes and, last, by co-magmatic red NNE-trending feldspar porphyry dykes and minor carbonatite dykes. All 44 sites of Table 1 (530 specimens, typically six drill-core samples per site). Pilot specimens were demagnetized by stepwise AF to 120 mT or thermally to 580 C; characteristic directions were isolated by AF cleaning to 15-20 mT or low-temperature demagnetization, followed by thermal demagnetization to at least 450 C, and fitted by least-squares lines and, where a viscous overprint overlapped, great circles. Site means required five or more specimens from at least three samples and alpha95 < 15 deg. Low-temperature demagnetization was found to create a spurious present-field-like remanence in some specimens, which were excluded at sites with a large viscous component. Eight sites carry the steep component ChRM A and six the shallow component ChRM B; the two are nowhere observed together. Site MB27 has a single ChRM-bearing specimen, and the other 29 sites gave no site mean, mostly because a recent viscous remanence carried by multidomain magnetite dominates. Directions are in geographic coordinates: the steep, variably striking dykes indicate that the complex has not been significantly tilted since emplacement. Baked-contact tests at sites MB13-14, MB15, MB16, MB22, MB26 and MB36 were inconclusive because the host syenite lacks a stable non-viscous remanence. Age of the rock: the weighted mean of the four hornblende 40Ar/39Ar plateau ages from sites MB44 (unit 1) and MB20 (unit 3), which the authors take as the intrusive age of the complex; the age range is its uncertainty, quoted at 2 sigma in Fig. 1 of the paper. Overlapping biotite and K-feldspar ages indicate rapid cooling after emplacement. Site coordinates are NAD-27 (Table 1).
