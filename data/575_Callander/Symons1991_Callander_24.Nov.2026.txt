The accepted sites are 16 radiating lamprophyre dikes, five syenitic-core sites (nepheline syenite and potassium trachyte dikes) and five sites in the fenite aureole; the three groups have indistinguishable means. The authors report that the complex has not been metamorphosed or tilted since emplacement (untilted aeromagnetic anomaly; flat-lying Middle Ordovician limestone on the nearby Manitou Islands complex), so directions are in situ. The A component is carried by magnetite (and hematite at the trachyte site 7 and in some fenite) with pseudo-single-domain to multidomain and single-domain grain sizes, and was isolated by alternating-field and thermal demagnetization with line fits and, at a few sites, remagnetization circles. The pole is the Fisher mean of the site virtual geomagnetic poles after unifying polarity, reported in the northern hemisphere at the antipode of the authors' 46.3 S, 121.4 E. A contact test with a lamprophyre dike (site 19) and Grenville granite gneiss (site 29) is positive: the gneiss 0.1 m from the contact is remagnetized with the dike direction, whereas 0.6-4.1 m away it retains an older hematite-borne B component, which the authors correlate with the ca. 980 Ma Grenville segment of the apparent polar wander path; the gneiss blocks are included as host-rock rows. The authors' reversal test is positive but, in their words, 'not robust' with three reversed sites. The age of the complex is estimated by the authors from K-Ar biotite and Pb-Pb mineral isochron dates; a concordant U-Pb zircon date of 577 +/- 1 Ma on nepheline syenite (S.L.
